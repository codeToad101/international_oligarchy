TRANSNATIONAL OLIGARCHIC CAPTURE: AN ABM/SD MODEL
====================================================
Project overview -- REVISED after the M0 build. Changes since the last
version: (a) the core question is reframed around which oligarchic
STRATEGIES (relationship structures, above all cooperation) are profitable
and when they can be attained AND maintained; (b) Country and Oligarch are
formally defined as the two core agents in model_core.py and every model
version (M0, M1, ...) is built on them; (c) goodwill now exists on EVERY
relationship (oligarch<->oligarch and state<->oligarch), replacing the
lone Alliance.goodwill; (d) Section 5 items resolved by M0 are marked, and
what M0 deliberately does NOT yet implement is listed explicitly.

FILES
  model_core.py   agent definitions (Country, Oligarch), Relations
                  (goodwill store), Params, World (init + capture contest)
  m0_model.py     M0 flow equations (M0World subclasses World) + diagnostics
  m0_notes.md     M0 specification details, sources, results snapshot

1. CORE QUESTION (THESIS)
--------------------------
Which oligarchic strategies are profitable -- and under what conditions
can an oligarch actually ATTAIN and MAINTAIN them -- when capture abroad
invites backlash, state collapse and asset seizure?

Strategy here is NOT "how much abroad" (a single ambition/footprint dial).
It is the shape of an oligarch's RELATIONSHIPS: who they make deals with
(inside vs. outside their own industry), how visible their own footprint
is in a host country, and how they stand with each host state. Working
hypotheses (to be tested, not assumed):
  H1. The oligarchs who stay oligarchs are those who make deals abroad
      without a visible footprint that "rubs people the wrong way" -- i.e.
      who keep good standing with host states.
  H2. The oligarchs who stay oligarchs are those who make deals with
      partners OUTSIDE their own industry (complementary rather than
      overlapping interests).
The aggregate consequence -- runaway consolidation (a few oligarchic
networks dominating multiple states) versus a competitive, multipolar
equilibrium -- is the second outcome of interest, and follows from which
strategies survive.

The explicit alternatives we are arguing against: (i) that consolidation
is inevitable once cross-border alliances are possible; (ii) that success
is determined simply by how far abroad an oligarch expands. The model needs
built-in counterbalancing forces (backlash, collapse, seizure) so that the
outcome is genuinely conditional on parameters, not baked into the
mechanism.

"Stays an oligarch" is operationalized as an EXIT RULE (own definition): an
oligarch exits if its wealth ever falls at least 50% below its own recorded
peak (Params.exit_drawdown). Without an exit rule the survival claim is
not measurable.

2. GOAL / SCOPE
-----------------
Target: publishable in an undergraduate research journal.

Framing: a minimal, clearly-explained mechanism and its conditional
behavior -- NOT a fully realistic, richly-calibrated model. We cannot
validate against real cross-national capture data (it barely exists in
usable form), so the paper's value has to come from structural plausibility
and sensitivity analysis (in the tradition of exploratory SD work like
World3/Limits to Growth), not point prediction. All numeric parameters are
currently PLACEHOLDERS tuned for structural plausibility (sane returns per
tick; collapse neither absent nor constant), not fitted to data.

Primary claim to demonstrate: which relationship-based strategies survive
is CONDITIONAL on institutional/backlash strength, not inevitable.
-> Primary parameter to sweep: backlash/detection sensitivity (gamma).

Secondary claims, if time allows:
  - origin-wealth asymmetry between allying oligarchs determines whether
    deals are genuinely mutual or let big players absorb small ones.
  - the open question of whether same-industry overlap breeds rivalry or
    affinity (Params.overlap_sign; switchable, see Section 4).

Future add-on (parked; to be returned to if time allows): passive vs.
ambitious populations -- comparing collapse and seizure outcomes across
populations that differ in how far abroad they expand.

Stretch goal, if time allows: adaptive oligarchs that learn to choose
between indoctrination-style investment (propaganda) and more cautious/
hedged investment, turning the static phase diagram into a strategic one.

3. THEORETICAL GROUNDING
--------------------------
(Entries marked [NEW] were added with M0. Page-level details of all
citations should be verified before use in the paper.)

- Winters, "Oligarchy" (2011) -- definitional anchor. Distinguishes RULING
  oligarchs (direct political power) from CIVIL oligarchs (power through
  legal/economic institutions -- property law, courts, lobbying). Our two
  oligarch types map directly onto this rather than inventing new
  vocabulary.

- "Company-state" concept (historically applied to the East India Company,
  extended to United Fruit/Chiquita in Central America) -- the sharper term
  for a civil oligarch/corporation exercising quasi-sovereign power abroad.

- Piketty, "Capital in the Twenty-First Century" -- the r > g engine behind
  the capital-accumulation stocks.

- Turchin's Political Stress Indicator (Secular Cycles; Ages of Discord;
  Historical Dynamics, 2003) -- borrowed as a STRUCTURAL template only
  (aggregate pressure computed top-down from a few macro drivers rather
  than summed from individual-citizen agents), not his calibrated
  coefficients, which are fitted to a different historical dataset.

- Post-Soviet 1990s privatization -- empirical precedent for collapse as a
  discrete, uneven reallocation event rather than smooth decay. Also the
  historical origin of the modern use of "oligarch."

- Charles Tilly, "War Making and State Making as Organized Crime" (1985) --
  protection-racket framing of extraction, useful comparative parallel for
  the capture mechanic.

- Tullock (1980) contest success functions (rent-seeking): a contestant's
  share of a prize is proportional to their investment raised to a
  "contest intensity" power, divided by the sum across all contestants'
  investments raised to that same power. Extended to DIVISIBLE prizes by
  Hillman & Riley (1989) / Hirshleifer (1989). Underlies how captured
  output in each (country, industry) is split among competing oligarchs
  (Params.tullock_r, currently r = 1).

- Hellman, Jones & Kaufmann, "Seize the State, Seize the Day" (World Bank,
  2000) -- empirical grounding for treating state capture as a real,
  measurable, BOUNDED quantity: even severe real-world capture never
  approaches 100%. Motivation for capture being a fraction of output with
  a ceiling strictly below 1 (Params.kmax = 0.85). Not calibrated to it.

- Schaefer (1954) / Gordon (1954), the Gordon-Schaefer fishery model (see
  also Clark, Mathematical Bioeconomics, 1990) -- harvest depends on the
  harvester's effort and the resource stock, NOT on a resource-specific
  "efficiency" trait. M0 uses the EXPONENTIAL-CATCH form of the same idea,
  pool * (1 - exp(-q * total_effort)) (Baranov 1918 origin; see Clark
  1990), which equals q * effort * stock at small effort but adds the
  diminishing returns that prevent all-in corner solutions.  [CHANGED]
  Gordon's open-access rent-dissipation result is the reason congestion
  matters here.

- Ostrom, Governing the Commons (1990) -- common-pool framing for
  Country.unclaimed_assets after a collapse (DEFERRED; not in M0).

- [NEW] McFadden (1974), "Conditional logit analysis of qualitative choice
  behavior"; McKelvey & Palfrey (1995), Games & Economic Behavior 10:6-38
  -- logit / noisy choice, used for how oligarchs allocate capital across
  (country, industry) pairs (Params.logit_lambda).

- [NEW] Salton & McGill (1983), Introduction to Modern Information
  Retrieval -- cosine similarity, used as the measure of industry-
  preference overlap between oligarchs and of alignment between an
  oligarch's preferences and a country's payoff profile (initial goodwill).

- [NEW] Axelrod (1984), The Evolution of Cooperation -- MOTIVATION ONLY for
  goodwill built by repeated joint activity and eroded by its absence. No
  formula taken.

- [NEW] Law (2015), Simulation Modeling and Analysis, 5th ed. -- common
  random numbers: fixed-shape draws from separate streams so comparisons
  across parameter values see the same shocks.

- Existing ABMs to build on / differentiate from: the OLIGO model (Mason &
  Sengupta, oligarchs + elections) and the Ukrainian oligarchic-economy ABM
  (arXiv, firm-network capture). Neither models cross-border capture or
  capital flight as linked decisions -- that combination is our gap.

- OWN ASSUMPTIONS, NOT FROM ANY PAPER (state this in the paper): the
  logistic collapse hazard; the partial-adjustment (persistent) capture
  stock; the capture-rent income stream; backlash accumulation/decay;
  flight and divestment rules; seizure fractions; AR(1) commodity-price
  shocks; the exit rule; ALL goodwill initialization formulas, update
  equations and effect sizes; the interpretation of state goodwill (see
  Section 5); and all numeric parameter values.

4. MODEL ARCHITECTURE
------------------------
Hybrid ABM/SD: Country and Oligarch are the TWO CORE AGENTS, defined in
model_core.py and used by every model version. Each agent holds its own
state (SD-style stocks with accumulation/decay) AND its own behavior
methods. Relationships are a shared object (Relations) that both agent
types reference, so the two ends of a relationship always see one value.

Baseline scope: 5 countries x 2 oligarchs per country = 10 oligarchs
(FIXED, not sampled, so seeds are comparable; the earlier 0-2 random
sampling made cross-seed variance depend on which countries got
oligarchs). Scaling beyond this is still to be tested.

AGENT 1: Country (the state)
  Fixed traits:
    baseline_wealth            scales resident oligarchs' starting capital
    institutional_resilience   makes capture harder for everyone and speeds
                               backlash decay
    collapse_threshold         backlash level at which the collapse hazard
                               reaches half its maximum
    industry_endowment[i]      comparative advantage (specialist/generalist
                               split; 2 specialty industries per country).
                               Governs pool size only, never extraction
                               efficiency
    payoff_profile[i]          endowment x price: the "potential payoffs"
                               that initialize goodwill
  Stocks / state:
    backlash_pressure          accumulated popular pressure (see FLOWS)
    recovery_left              ticks of reduced output after a collapse
    collapse_count
    industry_output[i]         this tick's output pool
    industry_capture[i]        total captured FRACTION of industry i's
                               output (all oligarchs)
    domestic_capture           output-weighted captured fraction of the
                               whole economy
  Behaviors: produce, update_backlash, hazard, check_collapse
  DEFERRED (kept on the class, unused in M0): unclaimed_assets,
    scarcity_pressure, wealth_returned_to_population

AGENT 2: Oligarch
  Fixed traits:
    oligarch_type              civil or ruling (ruling => home-capture
                               multiplier; static in M0)
    home_country
    industry_preferences[i]    shares summing to 1 (Dirichlet-sampled);
                               weight allocation choices
    risk_tolerance             maps to a flight/divest threshold on the
                               perceived backlash ratio B/theta
  Stocks / state:
    capital                    LIQUID wealth, deployable
    offshore_capital           safe from seizure, earns a small return,
                               one-way in M0 (flagged simplification)
    positions[c][i]            sunk, seizable stock per (country, industry).
                               In M0 this MERGES the old
                               industry_investments and illiquid_assets:
                               one stock drives BOTH extraction and the
                               capture contest, and it is what a collapse
                               seizes
    capture[c][i]              captured fraction of (c, i) output held by
                               this oligarch (a persistent stock that decays
                               without continued investment).
                               foreign_capture[c] is DERIVED from this, not
                               independently updated
    seized_total, initial_wealth, peak_wealth, max_drawdown
                               (exit bookkeeping)
  Behaviors: settle (income, costs, insolvency), suffer_collapse,
    flee_and_divest, net_attractiveness, deploy, record_wealth, exited
  INITIAL DOMESTIC INFLUENCE: each oligarch starts with a share
    (Params.init_home_frac) of its capital placed at home, and its capture
    stock starts at the contest-implied level -- so every oligarch has a
    nonzero initial domestic capture, as in the original brief.

RELATIONSHIPS: goodwill on EVERY relationship (Relations; SYMMETRIC for now)
  G[a,b]   oligarch <-> oligarch (O x O; G[a,b] == G[b,a]; zero diagonal)
  S[o,c]   state <-> oligarch, one value per relationship, INCLUDING the
           home country (O x C)
  Both in [-1, 1]. Directed goodwill (A's view of B differs from B's view
  of A) is PARKED as a later extension.

  Initialization ("industrial interests & potential payoffs"):
    S0 = home_bonus*home + s_scale*(alignment - mean alignment)
         - foreign_stigma*foreign,
         alignment = cosine(oligarch preferences, country payoff_profile)
    G0 = overlap_sign * init_overlap_coef * (cosine(pref_a, pref_b) - mean)
         + same_country_bonus * co_resident
    overlap_sign = -1: same-industry overlap breeds RIVALRY;
                   +1: it breeds AFFINITY. This is the open question from
    the old Alliance TODO; it is a switch, used in BOTH initialization and
    dynamics.
  Dynamics:
    G: reverts toward G0; rises with COMPLEMENTARY co-presence (same
       country, different industries); moves by overlap_sign with
       same-pool overlap; symmetric stochastic shocks
    S: reverts toward S0; rises with visible investment; falls with the
       captured share of output; a foreigner gains from "vouching" by
       liked local oligarchs (halo); stochastic shocks
  Effects:
    - capture effectiveness x (1 + zeta_cap*S)
    - capture effectiveness x (1 + eta * sum_b G_ab * presence_bc)
      (signed: rival presence hurts, partner presence helps)
    - backlash contribution of an oligarch's capture x (1 - zeta_bl*S),
      times a foreignness visibility factor
    - seizure loss on collapse x (1 - zeta_seize*max(S,0))
    - deployment attractiveness: + goodwill_pull*(S + tanh(partner presence))

PARAMETERS (heterogeneous traits sampled once at init; global constants in
Params in model_core.py)
  Extraction:  q (catchability), yield_scale, price_bar[i] (compressed to
               1.0-2.5 so specialization is not swamped by price),
               op_cost
  Capture:     kmax, kappa, capture_adj, capture_rent, tullock_r,
               ruling_home_mult. There is NO separate foreign-capture
               penalty: home advantage flows through goodwill (home S0 is
               high) plus the ruling multiplier
  Backlash:    gamma (PRIMARY SWEEP), b_decay, foreign_backlash_mult,
               shock_sd
  Collapse:    h_max, h_width, seize_frac, foreign_exposed,
               resident_liquid_hit, recovery_ticks, recovery_output
  Prices:      price_ar, price_sd
  Goodwill:    see RELATIONSHIPS above (home_bonus, s_scale,
               foreign_stigma, init_overlap_coef, same_country_bonus,
               overlap_sign, gw_decay_*, lam_*, halo, sigma_gw_*,
               zeta_*, eta, goodwill_pull)
  Behavior:    deploy_frac, logit_lambda, perc_sd, flight_gain/max,
               divest_gain/max, fire_sale_loss, offshore_return,
               exit_drawdown

FLOWS IMPLEMENTED IN M0 (m0_model.py, one tick, in order)
  1. prices: log price deviations follow an AR(1) process (shared across
     countries); Country.produce -> output pool = endowment * yield_scale *
     price, halved during recovery
  2. extraction: pool * (1 - exp(-q * total position)) split by Tullock
     share -> each oligarch's extraction revenue
  3. capture contest: aggregate capture in (c, i) saturates toward kmax as
     effective effort rises; split among oligarchs by Tullock share;
     each oligarch's capture stock partially adjusts toward its target
     (and decays without investment). Capture pays a separate CAPTURE RENT:
     capture_rent * captured share * output  [load-bearing assumption --
     see Section 5]
  4. settle: income, running costs, offshore return; insolvent oligarchs
     liquidate positions at a fire-sale loss
  5. backlash: Country.update_backlash from the capture LOAD (output-
     weighted capture, weighted by foreignness and (1 - zeta_bl*S)),
     decaying faster with institutional_resilience, plus an exogenous shock
  6. collapse: probabilistic hazard = h_max * logistic((B - threshold) /
     h_width). On collapse: backlash reset, recovery begins, positions
     there are seized (foreign holdings too if foreign_exposed), reduced by
     the polity's goodwill toward each oligarch; resident oligarchs also
     lose part of their liquid capital
  6b. goodwill updates (see RELATIONSHIPS)
  7. perception and protection: noisy perceived backlash -> capital flight
     (liquid -> offshore) and divestment of positions above the oligarch's
     risk threshold
  8. deployment: each oligarch allocates a share of liquid capital by a
     logit over ALL (country, industry) pairs with positive risk-adjusted
     marginal return (marginal extraction + marginal capture rent -
     running cost - expected seizure loss + relationship pull), weighted by
     industry preference
  9. record: wealth history; drawdown drives the exit rule

STOCHASTICITY (all from separate RNG streams, fixed-shape draws each tick =>
common random numbers): AR(1) price shocks; exogenous backlash shocks;
noisy risk perception; probabilistic collapse; goodwill shocks (symmetric
for G). Noise is deliberately NOT applied to accounting identities.

FLOWS DEFERRED (from the earlier version; not in M0)
  - resource stock, regeneration, abuse threshold, scarcity channel
    (old flows #9-10, #13; with the M0 calibration the abuse threshold is
    reachable again, since extraction fraction 1-exp(-qX) hits 0.5 at
    X = ln2/q ~ 2.3)
  - composite immiseration channel with wealth_returned_to_population (#11)
  - the illiquid vs. liquid split, unclaimed_assets and asset repurposing
    (#15-16), beyond the merged position stock
  - seizure of power / ruling_status transitions (#14)
  - deals as discrete events, pooled joint capital, defection (#5-6)
  - displacement grievance as an explicit flow (#8; PARTLY covered in M0 by
    the same-pool overlap term in goodwill dynamics)
  - propaganda, directed goodwill, passive-vs-ambitious populations

INITIALIZATION RULE
  Oligarch starting capital = home country baseline_wealth x U(0.5, 1.5) x
  capital_scale; type is ruling with probability ruling_prob (placeholder).
  baseline_wealth is decoupled from industry output values (noted side
  effect of endowment no longer playing triple duty). init_home_frac of
  capital is placed at home in proportion to preferences x home payoff
  profile. Initial goodwill per the formulas above. Backlash, recovery and
  capture (beyond the initial contest-implied level) start at 0.

5. OPEN QUESTIONS / DECISIONS STILL NEEDED
---------------------------------------------
RESOLVED BY M0 (revisit if results are sensitive):
  - Collapse form: a probabilistic hazard on backlash pressure
    (h_width -> 0 recovers a hard threshold). h_width is a sweepable
    parameter, so hard-vs-soft is testable rather than assumed.
  - Does collapse seize foreign holdings? M0: yes, controlled by
    Params.foreign_exposed (default True) -- this materially affects
    results and must be defended/swept.
  - Endowment/stock/value coupling: endowment sets pool size only; prices
    compressed; capture is a fraction of output (removes the earlier
    units mismatch between industry values and oligarch capital).
  - Relationship between industry investment and capture: one merged
    position stock feeds both extraction and the Tullock capture contest;
    capture is a saturating fraction (kmax = 0.85 kept); tullock_r = 1.
  - Where randomness enters: see STOCHASTICITY above.
  - Whether capture should boost extraction: M0 sidesteps it with a
    SEPARATE capture-rent stream (capture_rent). The catchability-boost
    version remains open.

STILL OPEN / NEW:
  - LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION: the capture-rent stream anchors oligarchs at
    home. Earlier (ambition-based) tests showed that removing it made
    all-in foreign expansion profitable. It needs its own justification
    and a sensitivity sweep before any strategy claim is made.
  - Meaning of STATE goodwill: M0 treats it as the POLITY'S standing toward
    the oligarch (legitimacy), which is why higher S reduces seizure and
    backlash. If it is meant to be REGIME favor, several effects need
    reconsidering. To be confirmed.
  - Deals: currently cooperation is emergent (goodwill + presence), not an
    explicit event. H1/H2 are testable properly only with discrete deals
    (pooled commitments per pair x country x industry) and defection.
    Still open: purely passive goodwill decay vs. an active "betrayal"
    event.
  - Same-industry overlap: rivalry or affinity (overlap_sign)? Early M0
    runs show only a marginal effect on exit rates; needs more work.
  - Survival measure: current partner-goodwill diagnostic averages over all
    oligarchs, including ones in unrelated countries; a measure restricted
    to partners present in shared countries is needed. Diagnostics are
    also endogenous (losing positions erodes goodwill), so causal
    language should be avoided until deals are explicit.
  - Symmetric vs. directed goodwill (parked).
  - Ruling status: static type in M0. Whether becoming ruling changes the
    rules going forward (cheaper domestic capture, higher backlash
    sensitivity) remains a dead end.
  - Offshore_capital is one-way in the baseline (never reinvested) --
    acceptable v1 simplification, flag it explicitly.
  - Scale: how many countries/oligarchs before ABM becomes the
    computational bottleneck; and whether results survive scaling from 10
    to 20-30 oligarchs (5-10 agents is thin for an "ABM").
  - Calibration: every parameter is a placeholder. Priority sensitivity
    sweeps: zeta_cap, zeta_bl, zeta_seize, eta, halo, lam_cap,
    capture_rent, seize_frac, h_width, exit_drawdown, plus gamma.
  - A single domestic "policy capture" stock per country vs. bilateral
    trade/diplomatic mechanics: out of scope, future work.
  - Deferred-mechanic questions carried over (relevant only if those
    mechanics return): what fraction of investment hardens into illiquid
    assets; whether ASSET_CLAIM_RATE should differ from catchability;
    whether scarcity_pressure is per-industry or aggregate; whether
    CATCHABILITY and price vary by oligarch; how immiseration's two
    components combine; the abuse-threshold and regeneration values.

6. NEXT STEPS
---------------
1. (DONE) M0: agents defined in model_core.py; M0 flows in m0_model.py;
   first diagnostics. Preliminary observations (placeholder parameters,
   descriptive only; see m0_notes.md): higher backlash sensitivity raises
   collapse frequency and exit rates as expected; survivors hold higher
   state goodwill abroad; foreign footprint size does not predict
   survival; no protective effect of oligarch-oligarch goodwill YET (the
   measure and the mechanism are both crude). None of this is a result.

2. Make deals explicit (M1 core): pooled commitments per (pair, country,
   industry), goodwill-modulated effectiveness, defection/betrayal. This
   is what turns H1/H2 into testable strategy claims.

3. Better survival analysis: partner-relevant goodwill measures
   (partners present in shared countries); footprint visibility measures;
   an explicit test of "deals with partners outside my industry."

4. Robustness/sensitivity sweeps (list in Section 5), starting with the
   capture-rent assumption, foreign-asset exposure and h_width; then the
   PRIMARY sweep of gamma across many seeds looking for the qualitative
   flip between strategies that survive and strategies that do not.

5. Scaling check (10 -> 20-30 oligarchs) and paired common-random-number
   comparisons across parameter values.

6. Reintroduce deferred mechanics (depletion/scarcity, illiquid vs.
   liquid, asset repurposing, ruling transitions) ONLY as ablations: does
   each change M0's conclusions?

7. If time allows: origin-wealth asymmetry sweep; directed goodwill;
   passive-vs-ambitious populations; adaptive learning (indoctrinate vs.
   hedge).

8. Write up: lead with the definitional grounding (Winters' civil/ruling
   split), state the gap relative to OLIGO/Ukrainian-ABM literature
   explicitly, present the strategy-survival result as the core finding,
   state which components are own assumptions, and list Section 5's
   unresolved items as limitations/future work rather than silently
   omitting them.
