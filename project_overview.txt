TRANSNATIONAL OLIGARCHIC CAPTURE: AN ABM/SD MODEL
====================================================
Project overview -- last updated after prof meeting, core-change: countries
are now agents in their own right, not passive SD-only objects.


1. CORE QUESTION (THESIS)
--------------------------
Under what conditions does transnational oligarchic capture -- via pooled
capital, alliances, and the civil-to-ruling transition -- tip into runaway
consolidation (a small number of oligarchic networks dominating multiple
states), versus settle into a competitive, multipolar equilibrium?

The explicit alternative we are arguing against: that capture consolidation
is inevitable once cross-border alliances are possible. The model needs at
least one built-in counterbalancing force (backlash/detection) so that the
outcome is genuinely conditional on parameters, not a foregone conclusion
baked into the mechanism.


2. GOAL / SCOPE
-----------------
Target: publishable in an undergraduate research journal.
Framing: a minimal, clearly-explained mechanism and its phase-transition
behavior -- NOT a fully realistic, richly-calibrated model. We cannot
validate against real cross-national capture data (it barely exists in
usable form), so the paper's value has to come from structural plausibility
and sensitivity analysis (in the tradition of exploratory SD work like
World3/Limits to Growth), not point prediction.

Primary claim to demonstrate: capture consolidation is CONDITIONAL on
institutional/backlash strength, not inevitable.
  -> Primary parameter to sweep: backlash/detection sensitivity.
Secondary claim, if time allows: origin-wealth asymmetry between allying
oligarchs determines whether alliances are genuinely mutual or let big
players absorb small ones.
Stretch goal, if time allows: adaptive oligarchs that learn to choose
between indoctrination-style investment and more cautious/hedged
investment, turning the static phase diagram into a strategic one.


3. THEORETICAL GROUNDING
--------------------------
- Winters, "Oligarchy" (2011) -- definitional anchor. Distinguishes RULING
  oligarchs (direct political power) from CIVIL oligarchs (power through
  legal/economic institutions -- property law, courts, lobbying). Our two
  oligarch types map directly onto this rather than inventing new
  vocabulary.
- "Company-state" concept (historically applied to the East India Company,
  extended to United Fruit/Chiquita in Central America) -- the sharper term
  for a civil oligarch/corporation exercising quasi-sovereign power abroad.
- Piketty, "Capital in the Twenty-First Century" -- the r > g engine behind
  the capital-accumulation stocks.
- Turchin's Political Stress Indicator (Secular Cycles; Ages of Discord) --
  borrowed as a STRUCTURAL template only (aggregate pressure = combination
  of a few macro drivers, computed top-down rather than summed from
  individual-citizen agents), not his calibrated coefficients, which are
  fitted to a different historical dataset.
- Post-Soviet 1990s privatization -- empirical precedent for collapse as a
  discrete, uneven reallocation event (assets suddenly redistributed to
  whoever is positioned to grab them) rather than smooth decay. Also the
  historical origin of the modern use of "oligarch."
- Charles Tilly, "War Making and State Making as Organized Crime" (1985) --
  protection-racket framing of extraction, useful comparative parallel for
  the capture mechanic.
- Tullock (1980) contest success functions (rent-seeking): a contestant's
  share of a prize is proportional to their investment raised to a
  "contest intensity" power, divided by the sum across all contestants'
  investments raised to that same power. Extended to DIVISIBLE prizes
  (shares of a captured value rather than a single winner) by Hillman &
  Riley (1989) / Hirshleifer (1989) -- this is the functional form
  underlying Country.industry_capture's split among competing oligarchs
  (see model_core.py CAPTURE_CONTEST_INTENSITY). Chosen specifically
  because it already handles the "new entrant investing in an
  already-captured industry" case: the incumbent's realized share drops
  purely from a rival's investment, without the incumbent changing
  anything -- a clean, already-computed trigger for retaliation/goodwill
  effects.
- Hellman, Jones & Kaufmann, "Seize the State, Seize the Day": State
  Capture, Corruption, and Influence in Transition (World Bank, 2000) --
  empirical grounding for treating "state capture" as a real, measurable,
  bounded quantity: in their study of post-Soviet transition economies,
  captor firms extracted large private benefits at real cost to the
  broader enterprise sector (overall sector growth reduced by roughly ten
  percentage points over three years) without the economy freezing
  entirely -- i.e. even severe real-world capture never approaches 100%.
  Used as justification for Country.industry_max_capture being a ceiling
  strictly below full industry value (MAX_CAPTURABLE_FRACTION), leaving a
  permanent independent/informal-sector floor.
- Schaefer (1954) / Gordon (1954), the classic bioeconomic "Gordon-
  Schaefer" fishery model (see also Clark, Mathematical Bioeconomics,
  1990) -- harvest is a function of the harvester's own effort and the
  current resource stock (harvest = catchability x effort x stock), NOT
  of a resource-specific "efficiency" trait belonging to whoever manages
  the resource. Adopted directly for how Oligarch.industry_investments
  translates into extracted resource value and profit
  (CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT, INDUSTRY_PRICE): removes an earlier design
  where a country-level "endowment" number played triple duty as stock
  size, extraction efficiency, AND synthetic GDP value. The same harvest
  shape is reused for post-collapse asset repurposing (ASSET_CLAIM_RATE)
  against Country.unclaimed_assets as the pool, rather than inventing a
  separate mechanism.
- Ostrom, Governing the Commons (1990) -- foundational framing for
  common-pool resources: a resource nobody holds exclusive title to,
  drawn down by whoever shows up with effort. Used as the conceptual
  grounding for Country.unclaimed_assets after a collapse: multiple
  oligarchs (domestic or foreign) investing in the same freshly-
  collapsed industry compete for the same shrinking, un-owned pool
  automatically via the shared harvest-shaped claim flow, without a
  bespoke contest or negotiation process -- deliberately kept light
  since simulating the internal governance of that scramble in detail
  is not this paper's focus.
- Existing ABMs to build on / differentiate from: the OLIGO model (Mason &
  Sengupta, oligarchs + elections) and the Ukrainian oligarchic-economy ABM
  (arXiv, firm-network capture). Neither models cross-border capture or
  capital flight as linked decisions -- that combination is our gap.


4. MODEL ARCHITECTURE
------------------------
Hybrid ABM/SD: Country and Oligarch are AGENTS (heterogeneous, make
discrete decisions, hold individual histories); the attributes on each
agent are SD-STYLE STOCKS governed by continuous accumulation/decay
equations. Alliances are a third, lightweight relational object between
oligarch pairs.

Baseline scope: 5 countries, 0-2 oligarchs per country (sampled at init).

STOCKS
  Oligarch.capital              -- LIQUID wealth only (illiquid split out
                                    below, since collapse treats them
                                    differently)
  Oligarch.illiquid_assets[c][i] -- physical/fixed capital (factories,
                                    offices, equipment) this oligarch
                                    holds in country c, industry i.
                                    Distinct from capital (liquid) and
                                    industry_investments (ongoing spend).
                                    Only this gets seized/frozen on a
                                    collapse event (see
                                    Country.unclaimed_assets below) --
                                    accumulation rule (how much of
                                    ongoing investment hardens into this)
                                    not yet decided
  Oligarch.offshore_capital     -- capital flight destination (one-way in
                                    baseline -- flagged simplification)
  Oligarch.foreign_capture[c]   -- influence held over foreign country c;
                                    DEFINED as the sum, across c's
                                    industries, of this oligarch's realized
                                    contest share of Country.industry_capture
                                    (see FLOWS #3 below) -- not an
                                    independently-updated stock
  Oligarch.ruling_status        -- 0 (civil) -> 1 (ruling), via seizure
  Oligarch.industry_investments[c][i] -- amount invested by this oligarch
                                    into industry i within country c
                                    (home country included -- this is how
                                    domestic investment is represented
                                    too). Feeds TWO separate mechanisms:
                                    (1) Gordon-Schaefer extraction (FLOWS
                                    #9) and (2) the capture contest
                                    (FLOWS #3). One stock currently drives
                                    both -- open question whether that
                                    should split into two.
  Country.domestic_capture      -- aggregate policy control within country;
                                    DEFINED as sum(industry_capture.values())
                                    once that flow is implemented
  Country.backlash_pressure     -- instability pressure, fed by THREE
                                    channels: immiseration, exclusion,
                                    scarcity (see FLOWS below)
  Country.industry_resource_stock[i] -- CURRENT extractable quantity of
                                    industry i's resource. Regenerates
                                    toward industry_resource_capacity each
                                    tick under normal use; only abusive
                                    extraction (see FLOWS #10) damages it
                                    permanently
  Country.industry_resource_capacity[i] -- the CEILING resource_stock
                                    regenerates toward -- a sustainable
                                    carrying capacity, not a one-time
                                    finite reserve. Starts equal to the
                                    initial stock; only shrinks from abuse
  Country.industry_value[i]     -- synthetic per-industry economic value
                                    (GDP-style proxy), used only to size
                                    industry_max_capture. Computed as
                                    industry_resource_capacity[i] *
                                    INDUSTRY_PRICE[i] -- i.e. from physical
                                    abundance and price, NOT from
                                    industry_endowment directly (see
                                    PARAMETERS below for why that changed)
  Country.industry_max_capture[i] -- ceiling on how much of industry_value
                                    can ever be captured (= industry_value
                                    * MAX_CAPTURABLE_FRACTION), leaving a
                                    permanent independent/informal-sector
                                    floor. Left as-is (0.85) for now --
                                    revisit later
  Country.industry_capture[i]   -- CURRENT total captured value in
                                    industry i, split among investing
                                    oligarchs via the capture contest.
                                    This is a DISTINCT mechanism from
                                    extraction (FLOWS #9) -- capture is
                                    about regulatory/policy control,
                                    extraction is about physical resource
                                    and profit. Open question: should a
                                    bigger captured share also boost an
                                    oligarch's effective extraction (a
                                    reinforcing loop directly relevant to
                                    the paper's thesis)?
  Country.wealth_returned_to_population -- cumulative wealth from
                                    capture/extraction that flowed back to
                                    the general population rather than
                                    being captured as oligarch capital.
                                    Feeds the immiseration channel
  Country.scarcity_pressure     -- backlash contribution specifically from
                                    ABUSIVE resource depletion (capacity
                                    damage, not ordinary extraction), kept
                                    separate from immiseration so the two
                                    drivers can be analyzed and swept
                                    independently
  Country.unclaimed_assets[i]   -- illiquid capital freed by a collapse
                                    event (every resident oligarch's
                                    illiquid_assets in that country are
                                    zeroed and moved here, per industry).
                                    Deliberately NOT automatically
                                    reclaimed -- claimed via the same
                                    Gordon-Schaefer harvest shape as
                                    resource extraction, against this
                                    pool instead (see PARAMETERS,
                                    ASSET_CLAIM_RATE); sits idle
                                    indefinitely if nobody invests
                                    (FLOWS #16)
  Alliance.goodwill             -- trust between two allied oligarchs.
                                    Can now also go NEGATIVE from a
                                    displacement grievance (see FLOWS #8)
                                    -- open question whether that requires
                                    a separate rivalry relation for
                                    non-allied competitors (Section 5).
                                    LEFT ALONE for now -- will likely
                                    revisit from a network perspective

PARAMETERS (heterogeneous, sampled once at init -- NOT stocks)
  Country.baseline_wealth, institutional_resilience, collapse_threshold,
  industry_endowment[i]         -- comparative advantage (0-1) of country
                                    in industry i; sampled so every
                                    country has 1-2 "specialty" industries
                                    (high endowment) and is mediocre/weak
                                    at the rest -- never uniformly good or
                                    bad across the board. Governs RESOURCE
                                    ABUNDANCE ONLY (how big
                                    industry_resource_stock/capacity start
                                    out) -- deliberately NOT an extraction
                                    efficiency multiplier anymore.
                                    Extraction efficiency is now a single
                                    global CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT shared
                                    by every oligarch everywhere (see
                                    model_core.py module docstring) --
                                    "better resources" now means "more of
                                    it," never "objectively better at
                                    extracting it." (See model_core.py for
                                    the current specialist/generalist
                                    sampling mechanism -- a placeholder
                                    split, not a smooth distribution.)
  CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT      -- global constant governing extraction:
                                    harvest = CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT *
                                    investment * resource_stock (Gordon-
                                    Schaefer bioeconomic harvest model --
                                    see Section 3). Same for every
                                    oligarch, every country, every
                                    industry in v1.
  INDUSTRY_PRICE[i]             -- per-industry price constant, used both
                                    to turn harvest into profit and to
                                    size industry_value (capacity * price).
                                    Deliberately varied by industry so a
                                    scarce-but-valuable industry and an
                                    abundant-but-cheap one can be told
                                    apart even at similar physical
                                    abundance -- placeholder relative
                                    values
  ASSET_CLAIM_RATE              -- global constant governing post-collapse
                                    asset repurposing: claim =
                                    ASSET_CLAIM_RATE * investment *
                                    Country.unclaimed_assets[i]. Same
                                    Gordon-Schaefer shape as extraction,
                                    reused against a different pool
                                    rather than inventing a third
                                    mechanism -- gets "nothing forces
                                    claiming to happen" for free. Claimed
                                    value then splits between the
                                    claimant's illiquid_assets (absorbed)
                                    and capital (stripped), linearly by
                                    risk_tolerance. Placeholder value
  CAPTURE_CONTEST_INTENSITY (r) -- Tullock contest-intensity for splitting
                                    industry_capture among competing
                                    oligarchs. Starting at r=1
                                    (proportional/"lottery") -- the
                                    field's standard default, chosen
                                    because direct empirical measurement
                                    of real rent-seeking/capture contests
                                    is notoriously hard to get, not
                                    because r=1 is a settled empirical
                                    finding. Candidate future refinement:
                                    tie r to institutional_resilience
                                    instead of a global constant -- some
                                    contest-success-function literature
                                    treats "discriminatory power" as an
                                    institutional-environment property
                                    (weak institutions -> money buys more
                                    decisive dominance -> higher r) rather
                                    than a fixed number. Not implemented.
  propensity_to_dissatisfaction (if kept separate from backlash inputs)

FLOWS (see model_core.py World.step() docstring for the authoritative list)
  1. return_on_capital        -> capital           (boosted by domestic_capture: R1 loop)
  2. capital_flight           -> capital -> offshore_capital
  3. capture_contest          -> industry_investments[c][i] determines each
     oligarch's realized share of industry_capture[i], two stages:
     (a) aggregate captured value in (c,i) saturates toward
     industry_max_capture[i] as total investment rises (diminishing
     returns, never reaches the ceiling); (b) that value is split among
     investing oligarchs via a Tullock-style contest share: x_o^r /
     sum_j(x_j^r), r = CAPTURE_CONTEST_INTENSITY. domestic_capture and
     foreign_capture[c] are then DEFINED as sums of industry_capture /
     realized shares, not independently updated (boosted by goodwill for
     joint investment: R2 loop)
  4. capture_decay            -> drains industry_capture absent continued
     investment (and therefore domestic_capture / foreign_capture)
  5. joint_capture_investment -> pooled capital -> foreign_capture
  6. joint_deals              -> foreign_capture activity -> goodwill
  7. goodwill_decay           -> drains goodwill
  8. displacement_grievance   -> when flow #3 causes an incumbent's
     realized share to drop because a rival increased their own
     investment, push goodwill negative (or an equivalent rivalry stock
     for non-allied pairs -- open question, LEFT ALONE for now) rather
     than only ever decaying passively
  9. resource_extraction      -> Gordon-Schaefer harvest per oligarch per
     (country, industry): harvest = CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT *
     industry_investments[c][i] * industry_resource_stock[i]; profit =
     INDUSTRY_PRICE[i] * harvest - industry_investments[c][i]. Draws down
     industry_resource_stock by the total harvest across all oligarchs
     there; splits profit between Oligarch.capital and
     Country.wealth_returned_to_population (split ratio TBD -- this is
     the "does the country still get the product of its own labor" lever)
  10. resource_regeneration   -> industry_resource_stock recovers toward
      industry_resource_capacity each tick UNLESS extraction that tick
      exceeded RESOURCE_ABUSE_THRESHOLD_FRACTION of the remaining stock,
      in which case industry_resource_capacity itself is permanently
      reduced instead (abuse damages the pool; ordinary use does not)
  11. immiseration channel    -> COMPOSITE of (a) the shortfall in
      wealth_returned_to_population relative to extraction volume and
      (b) the resource-consumption rate itself -> backlash_pressure.
      Exact combination (summed vs. one scaling the other) not yet
      decided (see Section 5). This absorbs the old "wealth_return_channel"
      -- it is not a separate channel, it IS what immiseration means here.
  12. exclusion channel       -> foreign_capture -> backlash_pressure
  13. scarcity_channel        -> industry_resource_capacity being damaged
      by abuse (flow #10) -> scarcity_pressure -> backlash_pressure.
      Independent trigger: a country can revolt from pure resource
      exhaustion even if extraction was otherwise "fairly" shared
  14. seizure                 -> domestic_capture -> ruling_status
      (suppressed by backlash_pressure: perceived risk of seizing power)
  15. collapse_event          -> backlash_pressure crosses collapse_threshold
      -> drains domestic_capture + resident oligarchs' liquid capital,
      sets Country.is_collapsed; every resident oligarch's illiquid_assets
      in that country are zeroed and their value moves to
      Country.unclaimed_assets per industry
  16. asset_repurposing       -> OPTIONAL: any oligarch (new entrant,
      survivor, or foreign investor already active here) MAY claim
      unclaimed_assets[i] via the SAME Gordon-Schaefer shape as
      extraction: claim = ASSET_CLAIM_RATE * industry_investments[c][i] *
      unclaimed_assets[i]. If nobody invests, claim = 0 and the pool sits
      idle indefinitely -- no forced flow, no decay/expiry (deliberately,
      to avoid over-modeling a state-internal process this paper isn't
      about). Claimed value splits between illiquid_assets (absorbed)
      and capital (stripped), linearly by risk_tolerance.

INITIALIZATION RULE
  Oligarch starting capital and starting oligarch_type are both functions
  of home country's baseline_wealth (richer countries -> better-capitalized
  oligarchs, and plausibly a different civil/ruling split). Current code
  uses placeholder distributions -- needs a deliberate functional form.
  Country industry_endowment/industry_resource_stock are sampled once at
  init per the specialist/generalist mechanism above -- also placeholder
  bounds/ratios. industry_resource_capacity starts equal to the initial
  resource stock; industry_value is then derived from capacity * price
  (NOT from baseline_wealth -- note this means a country's overall
  wealth level and its industries' GDP-proxy values are now fully
  decoupled, a side effect of removing endowment's old triple duty, worth
  keeping in mind). industry_capture and unclaimed_assets both start at 0
  for every industry.


5. OPEN QUESTIONS / DECISIONS STILL NEEDED
---------------------------------------------
- Collapse: hard threshold on backlash_pressure, or a probability that
  rises with pressure? (post-Soviet case suggests real-world behavior may
  be closer to a hard bifurcation)
- Alliance defection: purely passive goodwill decay, or also a discrete
  "betrayal" event an oligarch can actively choose?
- Ruling_status downstream effects: does becoming a ruling oligarch change
  the rules going forward (e.g. cheaper future domestic capture, but higher
  backlash sensitivity)? Currently a dead end once reached.
- Offshore_capital is one-way in the baseline (never reinvested) --
  acceptable simplification for v1, worth flagging explicitly as such.
- How many countries/oligarchs scale beyond the 5-country, 0-2-oligarch
  baseline before the ABM layer becomes the computational bottleneck.
- Whether a single domestic "policy capture" stock per country is enough,
  or whether bilateral trade/diplomatic mechanics need to be added (current
  answer: out of scope, flag as future work, not built by default).
- NEW: how does the granular Oligarch.industry_investments[c][i] stock
  relate to the existing aggregate Country.domestic_capture and
  Oligarch.foreign_capture[c] stocks? RESOLVED IN DIRECTION (not yet
  implemented): industry investment is the input to a Tullock-style
  capture contest (Tullock 1980; divisible-prize extension by Hillman &
  Riley 1989) whose output, industry_capture, saturates toward a
  GDP-linked ceiling (industry_max_capture) and is split among competing
  oligarchs by relative investment. domestic_capture and foreign_capture
  are then simply sums of industry_capture / realized shares.
  CAPTURE_CONTEST_INTENSITY (r): DECIDED for now -- r=1 (proportional),
  per the user's lean and consistent with the literature, which doesn't
  offer a settled empirical estimate for real-world capture contests
  (direct measurement is notoriously hard to get) and instead treats r=1
  as the field's standard tractable default. Candidate future
  refinement flagged in PARAMETERS above: tie r to
  institutional_resilience rather than leave it a flat constant.
  MAX_CAPTURABLE_FRACTION: left at 0.85 for now per the user -- revisit
  later.
- NEW: is the wealth-return channel a genuinely separate backlash input
  from immiseration, or is it a more precise/operational DEFINITION of
  what immiseration means? RESOLVED: yes, immiseration = wealth-return
  shortfall combined with resource consumption. Still open: whether the
  two combine by summing or by one scaling the other, and what the
  relative weighting should be.
- NEW: should industry_resource_stock/capacity's initial size be tied to
  industry_endowment, or should quality and quantity be sampled
  independently? RESOLVED: endowment now governs abundance ONLY
  (industry_resource_stock/capacity) and is explicitly no longer an
  extraction-efficiency dial -- extraction efficiency is a single global
  CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT (Gordon-Schaefer harvest model, see Section 3
  and PARAMETERS), the same for every oligarch/country/industry in v1.
  industry_value (the GDP-proxy sizing the capture ceiling) is now
  derived from industry_resource_capacity * INDUSTRY_PRICE[i] rather
  than from endowment, which resolves the earlier coupling problem
  (one number no longer sets stock size, efficiency, AND value at once).
  Side effect worth noting: industry_value is now fully decoupled from
  Country.baseline_wealth -- a country's overall wealth level no longer
  has any bearing on its industries' GDP-proxy values. Not flagged as a
  problem, just worth being aware of.
- NEW: does scarcity_pressure ever recover, or is resource depletion
  permanent once it happens? RESOLVED IN DIRECTION: ordinary extraction
  is fully recoverable (industry_resource_stock regenerates toward
  industry_resource_capacity each tick); only extraction that exceeds
  RESOURCE_ABUSE_THRESHOLD_FRACTION of the remaining stock in a single
  tick counts as "abuse" and permanently damages industry_resource_capacity
  itself. Still open: what RESOURCE_ABUSE_THRESHOLD_FRACTION and
  RESOURCE_REGEN_RATE should be (both placeholders), and whether the
  abuse threshold should itself vary by country (e.g. institutional_
  resilience making abuse less likely to cause lasting damage) rather
  than being a single global constant.
- NEW: is scarcity_pressure meant to be genuinely independent per
  industry, or does it aggregate to a single country-level number the
  moment it feeds backlash_pressure? Current code stores it as one
  aggregate float per country (Country.scarcity_pressure) even though
  industry_resource_stock/capacity are tracked per industry -- worth
  confirming that's the intended level of resolution.
- Displacement-grievance mechanic (Alliance.goodwill going negative from
  a rival displacing an incumbent's captured share): PARKED per the user
  -- likely to be revisited later from a network perspective rather than
  resolved now. No action needed until then.
- NEW: extraction is now genuinely oligarch-based (Gordon-Schaefer:
  harvest = CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT * investment * stock), but two
  related design questions remain open: (1) should CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT
  and/or INDUSTRY_PRICE vary by industry only, or also by oligarch (e.g.
  a risk_tolerance- or experience-linked catchability), which would
  reintroduce heterogeneity but at the OLIGARCH level rather than the
  country level; (2) does a bigger industry_capture share (regulatory/
  policy control) also boost an oligarch's effective catchability (i.e.
  captured political influence buys preferential resource access) --
  this would be a reinforcing loop directly relevant to the paper's
  thesis (capture enabling more capture) and is worth deliberately
  deciding rather than leaving the two mechanisms accidentally
  independent.
- NEW: illiquid asset / collapse repurposing mechanic added this round
  (Oligarch.illiquid_assets, Country.unclaimed_assets). Claiming
  mechanism RESOLVED: reuses the Gordon-Schaefer harvest shape against
  unclaimed_assets as the pool (ASSET_CLAIM_RATE), with claimed value
  split between illiquid_assets (absorbed) and capital (stripped)
  linearly by risk_tolerance -- deliberately reusing existing machinery
  and an existing trait rather than adding a bespoke contest or a new
  agent attribute, to keep this from turning into a full state-internal
  ABM (not this paper's focus). Access is open to ANY oligarch including
  foreign ones already investing in that country -- a domestic collapse
  becoming a cross-border capture opportunity falls out for free and is
  directly thesis-relevant. Deliberately NOT built: decay/expiry of
  unclaimed assets left untouched, or any preferential-access/priority
  rule for locals vs. foreigners -- both would add state-internal detail
  this paper doesn't need. Still open: (1) what fraction of ongoing
  Oligarch.industry_investments hardens into illiquid_assets over time
  in the first place, vs. staying liquid/extractable capital (i.e. the
  OTHER side of the illiquid_assets stock, not the collapse/claim side);
  (2) whether ASSET_CLAIM_RATE should differ from CATCHABILITY_COEFFICIENT
  in more than just placeholder value -- claiming built assets and
  harvesting raw resources may not really be the same "difficulty."


6. NEXT STEPS
---------------
1. Resolve remaining Section 5 open questions -- especially collapse
   functional form, the illiquid-asset accumulation/claiming mechanics,
   and whether industry_capture should feed back into extraction
   catchability -- these affect both Country.step logic and how "runaway
   consolidation" is even measured. CAPTURE_CONTEST_INTENSITY (r=1) and
   MAX_CAPTURABLE_FRACTION (0.85) are decided/parked for now.
2. Implement World.step() flow equations against model_core.py's stub,
   including the capture contest (flow #3), Gordon-Schaefer extraction
   (#9-10), the composite immiseration channel (#11), and the collapse ->
   unclaimed_assets -> optional repurposing sequence (#15-16).
3. Implement alliance formation/matching logic (currently no code decides
   WHICH oligarchs ally with which -- needs a rule, e.g. proximity in
   capital, shared target country, or random matching with acceptance
   probability). Note the existing TODO in model_core.py's Alliance class
   about comparing industry interests between allying oligarchs -- this
   is now easier to formalize since industry_investments is per-country.
4. Run the primary parameter sweep: backlash/detection sensitivity, low to
   high, across many seeds. Look for a qualitative flip between
   "consolidation" and "collapse resets the board" outcomes.
5. If time allows: origin-wealth asymmetry sweep (secondary claim).
6. If time allows: adaptive oligarch learning rule (indoctrinate vs. hedge)
   as a stretch extension.
7. Write up: lead with the definitional grounding (Winters' civil/ruling
   split), state the gap relative to OLIGO/Ukrainian-ABM literature
   explicitly, present the sweep result as the core finding, and list
   Section 5's unresolved items as limitations/future work rather than
   silently omitting them.
